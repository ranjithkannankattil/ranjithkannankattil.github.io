Cigna and direct billing

  • Cigna has no public, fixed list of "verified" Milan hospitals; the network changes. Check each provider in your own Cigna portal before booking: Cigna Envoy (employer plans) or the Cigna Global app (individual plans). Providers that bill Cigna directly carry a "direct settlement may be available" note.
  • For planned care, ask Cigna for a guarantee of payment in advance; without it, private hospitals usually ask you to pay first and claim back.
  • Large private groups that commonly hold international insurer agreements: Gruppo San Donato (San Raffaele, Galeazzi), Humanitas, MultiMedica. Confirm with the hospital's international patients office and Cigna both.
  • Keep every invoice (fattura) and report; claims and Italian tax deductions both need them.

Other ways to get covered

  • Employer health funds: many Italian contracts include a fondo sanitario integrativo (for example FASI for managers, Metasalute for metalworkers) that refunds private visits. Ask HR what yours is.
  • International expat plans: Cigna Global, Allianz Care, Bupa Global, AXA Global Healthcare and April International. Useful if you travel a lot or want care in several countries.
  • Italian private policies: Generali, UniSalute, Allianz Italia, Poste Assicura. Cheaper, Italy-only, often with waiting periods and limits on pre-existing conditions.
  • For a visa, the insurance must cover Italy for the whole stay (often at least €30,000) with no excess on emergencies; travel insurance is usually rejected.
  • Private visits without insurance are affordable by European standards: about €100–200 for a specialist in Milan. Centro Medico Santagostino is a cheaper private option.
