The one-year clock starts at your residenza date. Driving on a non-convertible foreign licence after that brings a fine of roughly €400–1,600 and confiscation. The theory test is only in Italian (since 2011). A driving school (autoscuola) package typically costs €800–2,000. Many expats start the theory course in month 6.

Owning a car: compulsory third-party insurance (RC auto), annual road tax (bollo), roadworthiness test (revisione) every 2 years after the first 4.

Driving in Milan: Area B and Area C

  • Area B covers almost the whole city. Mon–Fri 07:30–19:30 older polluting vehicles are banned (no ticket; you either qualify or you don't). Wrong entry: €83 fine plus fees.
  • Area C is the historic centre inside the Bastioni ring: a €7.50 daily ticket for most cars, linked to your number plate. Some 2026 sources report the charge being extended to weekends; check the Comune page before you drive in.
  • Check your plate on the Comune website before buying any used car. Diesel and older petrol cars face rolling bans.
  • Parking: blue lines are paid (EasyPark and similar apps), yellow lines are reserved for residents or permit holders, white lines are free.

Getting an Italian licence, step by step

  1. Enrol with an autoscuola (driving school), or apply privately at the Motorizzazione. Schools handle the paperwork and booking.
  2. Get a medical certificate from an authorised doctor (eyesight and general fitness), usually arranged by the school.
  3. Pass the theory quiz: 30 true/false questions in 20 minutes, max 3 mistakes, in Italian only. Practise with free quiz apps and the official question bank.
  4. You then get the foglio rosa (learner permit) and have up to two attempts at the practical test within 12 months. A set number of lessons with the school, including motorway and night driving, is compulsory.
  5. Pass the practical test and the licence is posted to you. New drivers have lower speed limits and power limits for the first years.

Owning a car

  • RC auto (third-party insurance) is compulsory; compare on Facile.it or Segugio.it. Your no-claims history from abroad usually doesn't count.
  • Bollo: yearly road tax paid to the Region (Lombardy), even if the car sits unused.
  • Revisione: technical check after 4 years, then every 2.
  • Winter tyres or chains are compulsory on many roads from 15 November to 15 April, especially towards the mountains.
  • Speed cameras (autovelox, Tutor on motorways) are everywhere; fines arrive by post months later and rise if paid late.
  • Car sharing (Enjoy, Share Now, Zity) and longer rentals are often cheaper than owning in the city.
