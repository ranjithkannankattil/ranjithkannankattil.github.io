Why the landlord matters

Four procedures depend on papers only the owner of your flat can give you: registering residence, the housing-suitability certificate (idoneità alloggiativa) needed to bring family, the police hosting declaration, and your permit applications and renewals. Ask for everything below before you sign. A landlord who won't provide them can block your family's visas for months.

Put it in the contract. Add a clause saying the owner will supply the floor plan, system certificates and a signed consent for family members to live there and take residence. Many Milan listings say no residenza; skip those if your family is coming.

Housing suitability certificate (idoneità alloggiativa)

To bring a spouse, children or parents, the Prefettura needs proof your home is big enough and healthy. In Milan the Comune issues an attestazione di idoneità abitativa, handled by the Municipio where the flat is (find it on the Comune's Geoportale).

  • You submit: the request form, your ID and permit, the registered contract, the floor plan, system certificates where asked, two €16 marche da bollo, and a delegation form if someone applies for you.
  • Applications are handled in order of arrival, with no urgent procedure. Apply as soon as the lease is registered.
  • Some flats, including those under 28.8 m², need an inspection or a technician's hygiene report. Private technical studios prepare the whole file for about €180–350 plus VAT.

National health standard (DM 5 July 1975): 14 m² each for the first four people, 10 m² for each one after. Usable area excludes walls, balconies and cellars. Bedrooms need at least 9 m² for one person and 14 m² for two, with an opening window. Count your family before choosing a flat.

Hosting declaration within 48 hours

  • Anyone who gives housing to a non-EU citizen, even a relative, must file a dichiarazione di ospitalità (also called cessione di fabbricato) within 48 hours of their arrival.
  • As a tenant, your landlord usually files it for you. When your spouse or parents arrive and live with you, you file it for them.
  • Attach a copy of your ID, the guest's passport and the registered contract. Get it stamped at the Comune, local police or a Commissariato, then send it to the Questura's immigration office by PEC, registered letter or in person.
  • Missing it can bring a fine of €500–3,500, and Questura files often ask for a copy.

Registering family residence

  • Once family arrive on their visas, register them at the Anagrafe at your address with a dichiarazione di residenza. Bring their passports, permits or post-office receipts, and apostilled, translated marriage and birth certificates so they are recorded as your family.
  • If a family member isn't named on the lease, the Anagrafe can ask for the owner's consent letter and ID.
  • A local police officer will visit to confirm everyone lives there. Make sure names are on the doorbell and letterbox.
  • Residence then unlocks each person's health card, ID card and school enrolment.
